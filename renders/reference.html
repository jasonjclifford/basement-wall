Which wall is which

Standing in either room facing the divider, "left" and "right" flip between rooms — the drawings on this site call the two walls by what they actually are instead, and every page below uses these names consistently:

  • External wall — the foundation wall. This is the bedroom's left wall and the office's right wall — the same physical wall, seen from opposite ends of the opening. The office Alex/desktop run's tall-Alex end sits flush against this wall.
  • Internal wall — the interior partition wall. This is the bedroom's right wall and the office's left wall — also the same physical wall. The bedroom desk sits flush against this wall; the office run's desk end trails toward it but stops ~12" short.

Materials estimate

  • 3/4" plywood: 3 sheets (2 if the wall cleats are ripped to 1x4 pine instead)
  • 1/2" plywood, paint-grade (birch/poplar core): 1 sheet — upper panel (switched from MDF for basement moisture tolerance; kept at 1/2", not dropped to the backers' 1/4", because the panel spans unsupported between cleats with no backing behind it)
  • 1/4" plywood, paint-grade (birch/poplar core): 3 sheets — backers
  • 1x3 primed pine: ~4 sticks of 8' — 3 stile battens + 2 backer center battens (5 total, down from 6: the backers' old outer-edge battens are now the stile battens, shared rather than duplicated)
  • 1x2 primed pine: ~4 sticks — upper panel perimeter/ceiling trim plus the 2 upper-panel seam cap strips
  • Baseboard: ~18 linear feet, profile to match each room's existing baseboard
  • Wall cleats: rip from 3/4" plywood offcuts, or 1x4 pine if that's easier to source

Fastening decisions

  • Wall cleat material: 3/4" plywood, ripped into strips — matches the ladder frame's own material and thickness, uses offcuts, and is fully hidden in the 3/4" gap so exposed plywood edges don't matter. 1x4 pine (finished dimension 3/4" x 3 1/2") is the documented fallback if ripping plywood isn't worth it.
  • Cleat to wall: screwed into studs (construction screws, not nails) — long enough to bite 1 1/4"+ into the stud through the cleat's 3/4" thickness. Lags/screws into studs and joists are acceptable for this "non-permanent" build.
  • Cleat to stile (decided): screwed through the stile's face into the cleat, driven from the room side before the backer panel goes on — not through the cleat from inside the 3/4" gap. See the ladder framing page's wall support detail for the fastening diagram. Tool-free stile removal isn't a design goal — the ceiling cleats and wall lags are already the "non-permanent" mechanism.
  • Upper panel cleats: 3/4" x 1 1/2" stock, both faces, at the top plate and again at the ceiling (four runs total).
  • Stile trim (decided): each of the 3 stiles gets one 1x3 batten centered on it, matching the backer battens' width — one shared piece per stile rather than separate battens on each adjacent backer edge, since every backer edge lands exactly at a stile. See the bedroom/office elevations.
  • Upper panel seams (decided): each of the 2 seams between panel pieces gets a 1x2 pine cap strip on the room-facing side, over the internal cleat splice — same logic as the batten covering the 4x4's board seam, applied to the ceiling panel.
  • Track light junction box (decided): the track runs perpendicular to the divider (front-to-back through the opening, office ceiling into the bedroom ceiling a short distance), positioned along the opening's width near the ceiling step down to the 9" duct soffit — standing in the bedroom facing the divider, that's the right side of the tall-ceiling zone, not near either the external or internal wall. The box is on the bedroom side. Rather than cutting an opening in the upper panel or biasing the whole panel toward one room, the panel's edge (in depth, only at this width-position) is placed just past the box on the bedroom side — leaving the box exposed and accessible in the open bedroom-ceiling area beyond the panel, satisfying NEC 314.29 with no cutout/curb/trim needed, and locally (not along its whole length) biasing the panel slightly toward the bedroom side there. The bare track beyond the box continues a short way further and pulls through that same gap if the divider is ever removed. See prompt.md's "Track light" section and photos/track-light.jpg. Exact placement in the drawings is pending a field measurement of the box/track's position and projection.

Finish

  • Bedroom-facing surfaces (4x4 backer, board & batten): painted very light pink. KALLAX units on the bedroom side: white.
  • Office-facing surfaces (4x3 backer, board & batten): color still undecided.
  • Baseboard: matches each room's existing profile, run across the bottom plate edge.
  • Nothing gets mounted to the backers — posters taped only, no anchors or hooks.

Resolved planning questions

  • Sound isolation: no second layer on the upper panel — the office side can stay quiet on the rare occasion someone's sleeping in the bedroom.
  • Removability: ceiling cleats into joists and lags into wall studs are acceptable — "non-permanent" doesn't require tool-free disassembly.
  • Floor type: linoleum, measured pretty level across the run (a slight wobble on the 4x4 noted — shim if needed when setting the bottom plate).
  • Outlets/switches/HVAC: none inside the divider's footprint or the soffit.
  • Room connectivity: each room already has its own separate door, so closing this opening doesn't trap anyone.

Open items

  • Open: Track light junction box — measurement only. The design approach is decided (see "Fastening decisions" below); what's still needed is the field measurement of the box/track's position and projection past the wall plane, to place the panel opening's exact position in the drawings.
  • Open: office backer paint color (currently drawn neutral/undecided in the elevations).

Measurement notes

Field measurements (KALLAX units already in place against the walls) turned up a few small differences from the original nominal numbers. None of them threaten the fit — the assembly still clears the opening with real margin — but they're worth knowing about:

  • Both KALLAX units measure slightly larger than the IKEA spec sheet: 4x3 is 44" wide (spec: 43 7/8"), 4x4 is up to 57 13/16" wide at the bottom of the carcass (spec: 57 7/8"), and both are 15 7/16" deep (spec: 15 3/8") and up to 57 11/16" tall (spec: 57 5/8"). The cut list and every drawing on this site use these measured numbers.
  • Inside-casing width was confirmed at 104", then re-measured more precisely at the floor (between the baseboards) at 104 3/16" — this site uses 104 3/16" as the more trustworthy reading.
  • Wall-to-wall opening width was directly measured at 106 1/8" (at KALLAX height), and independently cross-checked by butting both KALLAX units together against one wall and measuring the leftover gap at the other end — that cross-check implied ~106 1/16"–106 1/8", confirming the direct reading. This is about 2" wider than the casing numbers alone would suggest (104 3/16" inside casing + two 3/4"-proud casings = 105 11/16"), most likely because the casing isn't perfectly parallel to the wall face, or the casing-proud measurement itself was imprecise.
  • The 3/4" side gap (wall to stile) is being kept at the designed 3/4" on each side, not widened to use the full measured slack (~1" per side would be available) — the small extra beyond 3/4" is just uneven reveal to absorb during install, not a dimensioned part of the design.
  • Ceiling step measured at 28 3/4" from the external wall (design used 28 1/2"); tall-side ceiling height 82 1/8" (design: 82"); low-side ceiling height 73 1/4"–73 1/2" across a few points (design: 73") — this site uses the low end (73 1/4") as the worst case.
  • Floor is pretty level across the run; the 4x4 wobbles slightly — check and shim when setting the bottom plate.
  • Still open / not yet measured: wall stud-to-drywall-face thickness (needed to know how far the assembly projects into each room), stud locations on both walls, and the track light junction box's projection past the wall plane.
