What this is

We want to close the opening between the bedroom and the office with a divider that reads as a wall, built around two IKEA KALLAX bookcases we already own — one opening into each room, backed so you can't see or shine light through.

What matters: it has to block light (someone sleeps on one side while the other side is in use), feel solid when you lean on it, and be removable later without rebuilding the room. Screws into studs and joists are fine. Soundproofing is not needed.

These drawings show the plan, not a cut sheet. Re-measure everything and use your own numbers. If you'd build any of it differently, we're glad to hear it — we're only firm on the three goals above.

Start with the build reference — it has the cut list, materials, and the few things still to be decided.

How it goes together

  • A plywood ladder frame (bottom plate, three stiles, top plate) fills the opening and screws to the wall studs at each side.
  • The two KALLAX units drop into the bays as infill — they carry no load. Each bay is cut slightly wider than its unit so the boxes aren't a press fit.
  • Each unit gets a thin plywood backer on the face opposite its open side, then board-and-batten trim, with a horizontal cap rail across the top.
  • A plywood panel closes the gap from the bookcases up to the ceiling, centred in the depth so there's a usable ledge on both sides.
  • Two ceiling brackets at the centre stop the middle from wobbling — the wall screws only hold the two ends.
