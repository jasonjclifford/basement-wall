Which wall is which

Standing in either room facing the divider, "left" and "right" flip between rooms — the drawings on this site call the two walls by what they actually are instead, and every page below uses these names consistently:

  • External wall — the foundation wall. This is the bedroom's left wall and the office's right wall — the same physical wall, seen from opposite ends of the opening. The office Alex/desktop run's tall-Alex end sits flush against this wall.
  • Internal wall — the interior partition wall. This is the bedroom's right wall and the office's left wall — also the same physical wall. The bedroom desk sits flush against this wall; the office run's desk end trails toward it but stops ~12" short.

Materials estimate

  • 3/4" plywood: 3 sheets (2 if the wall cleats are ripped to 1x4 pine instead) — includes the top-plate splice scab and the upper-panel vertical blocking, both added by the structural review below
  • 1/2" plywood, paint-grade (birch/poplar core): 1 sheet — upper panel (switched from MDF for basement moisture tolerance; kept at 1/2", not dropped to the backers' 1/4", because the panel spans unsupported between cleats with no backing behind it)
  • 1/4" plywood, paint-grade (birch/poplar core): 3 sheets — backers
  • 1x3 primed pine: ~4 sticks of 8' — 3 stile battens + 2 backer center battens (5 total, down from 6: the backers' old outer-edge battens are now the stile battens, shared rather than duplicated)
  • 1x2 primed pine: ~4 sticks — upper panel perimeter/ceiling trim plus the 2 upper-panel seam cap strips
  • Baseboard: ~18 linear feet, profile to match each room's existing baseboard
  • Wall cleats: rip from 3/4" plywood offcuts, or 1x4 pine if that's easier to source

Fastening decisions

  • Wall cleat material: 3/4" plywood, ripped into strips — matches the ladder frame's own material and thickness, uses offcuts, and is fully hidden in the 3/4" gap so exposed plywood edges don't matter. 1x4 pine (finished dimension 3/4" x 3 1/2") is the documented fallback if ripping plywood isn't worth it.
  • Cleat to wall: screwed into studs (construction screws, not nails) — long enough to bite 1 1/4"+ into the stud through the cleat's 3/4" thickness. Lags/screws into studs and joists are acceptable for this "non-permanent" build.
  • Cleat to stile (decided): screwed through the stile's face into the cleat, driven from the room side before the backer panel goes on — not through the cleat from inside the 3/4" gap. See the ladder framing page's wall support detail for the fastening diagram. Tool-free stile removal isn't a design goal — the ceiling cleats and wall lags are already the "non-permanent" mechanism.
  • Top plate to stiles (decided): 3 or 4 #8 x 2" screws driven through each stile face into the plate at every joint (left, center, right), glued. This is good practice for the joint itself but is not what makes the frame tip-resistant — see "Structural review findings" below for why the top-plate splice needs its own reinforcement and the center span needs a ceiling connection regardless of how well this joint is fastened.
  • Top plate splice (decided): a 3/4" plywood scab, ~16" long, glued and screwed across the splice joint on top of the plate — see "Structural review findings" for why the bare butt splice over the center stile isn't enough on its own.
  • Upper panel cleats: 3/4" x 1 1/2" stock, both faces, at the top plate and again at the ceiling (four runs total).
  • Upper panel vertical blocking (decided): a 3/4" x 1 1/2" vertical run from the top plate to the ceiling cleat behind each of the 2 seams, plus a third run at the center stile's x-position (not a seam — added purely to carry the ceiling brackets below, since the center stile itself stops at the top plate like the other two and there's otherwise nothing between the top plate and the ceiling there). See "Structural review findings" for why the panel needs the seam blocking and geometry.js's CENTER_BLOCKING_X for the third run's position.
  • Center-stile ceiling brackets (decided): two steel angle brackets (2"x2"x1 1/2", 16-gauge), one per face, at the top of the center-line blocking run described above — not at the top of the center stile itself, which stops well short of the ceiling. Fastened to a ceiling joist if one lands within ~6" of center, or to toggle-bolt-rated drywall anchors otherwise (this is a tipping-restraint connection, not load-bearing — anchors rated 50 lb+ shear are adequate). See "Structural review findings" for why the end wall cleats alone don't cover the center span, and for the corrected force path.
  • Stile trim (decided): each of the 3 stiles gets one 1x3 batten centered on it, matching the backer battens' width — one shared piece per stile rather than separate battens on each adjacent backer edge, since every backer edge lands exactly at a stile. See the bedroom/office elevations.
  • Upper panel seams (decided): each of the 2 seams between panel pieces gets a 1x2 pine cap strip on the room-facing side, over the internal cleat splice and the vertical blocking behind it — same logic as the batten covering the 4x4's board seam, applied to the ceiling panel. Seam positions were moved to land at the ceiling step and at the low-zone's mid-span (see "Structural review findings") so every panel piece is a plain rectangle.
  • Track light junction box (decided): the track runs perpendicular to the divider (front-to-back through the opening, office ceiling into the bedroom ceiling a short distance), positioned along the opening's width near the ceiling step down to the 9" duct soffit — standing in the bedroom facing the divider, that's the right side of the tall-ceiling zone, not near either the external or internal wall. The box is on the bedroom side. Rather than cutting an opening in the upper panel or biasing the whole panel toward one room, the panel's edge (in depth, only at this width-position) is placed just past the box on the bedroom side — leaving the box exposed and accessible in the open bedroom-ceiling area beyond the panel, satisfying NEC 314.29 with no cutout/curb/trim needed, and locally (not along its whole length) biasing the panel slightly toward the bedroom side there. The bare track beyond the box continues a short way further and pulls through that same gap if the divider is ever removed. See prompt.md's "Track light" section and photos/track-light.jpg. Exact placement in the drawings is pending a field measurement of the box/track's position and projection.

Light sealing

Blocking light is a primary goal of this build (sound is explicitly not). The wall plane itself is handled by the backers and the upper panel, but light travels through the perimeter gaps, and until now none of them had a specified treatment. All four locations below want a bead of paintable latex caulk or a compressible foam strip at install — cheap, invisible once painted, and far easier to do during assembly than afterwards.

  • Under the bottom plate. The floor is level across most of the run but the 4x4 wobbles slightly, so shimming will leave gaps — and light travels along floors very effectively. Run a foam strip or caulk bead under the plate as it is set, before it is fastened.
  • At the ceiling. Drywall ceilings are never flat over 100"+, so the upper panel's top edge will show a light line however carefully the cleat is set. The 1x2 ceiling trim in the materials list covers this — it should be understood as a light seal, not just trim, and caulked to the ceiling rather than only nailed.
  • Both side gaps. The wall cleat fills the gap but is a friction fit against wall and stile with no sealing of its own. Caulk both sides, full height, where the cleat meets the wall and where the stile meets the cleat.
  • The upper panel's seams and its joint to the top plate. Each seam already gets a 1x2 cap strip on the room-facing side, which handles the visible gap; the joint down onto the top plate wants a caulk bead on both faces.

One consequence worth stating: the KALLAX shelves themselves are open cubbies, and each unit's backer only closes the face opposite its open side. Light entering an open cubby from one room stops at that unit's backer — so the assembly is opaque overall — but the cubbies will glow slightly from whichever room they open into. That is inherent to using open bookcases as the wall and is not a defect to fix.

Finish

  • Bedroom-facing surfaces (4x4 backer, board & batten): painted very light pink. KALLAX units on the bedroom side: white.
  • Office-facing surfaces (4x3 backer, board & batten): color still undecided.
  • Baseboard: matches each room's existing profile, run across the bottom plate edge.
  • Nothing gets mounted to the backers — posters taped only, no anchors or hooks.

Resolved planning questions

  • Sound isolation: no second layer on the upper panel — the office side can stay quiet on the rare occasion someone's sleeping in the bedroom.
  • Removability: ceiling cleats into joists and lags into wall studs are acceptable — "non-permanent" doesn't require tool-free disassembly.
  • Floor type: linoleum, measured pretty level across the run (a slight wobble on the 4x4 noted — shim if needed when setting the bottom plate).
  • Outlets/switches/HVAC: none inside the divider's footprint or the soffit.
  • Room connectivity: each room already has its own separate door, so closing this opening doesn't trap anyone.

Structural review findings

An independent structural/design review (Sept 2026) checked the whole plan for soundness, internal consistency, and constructability before this went to a handyman. Findings below, grouped by what's been resolved and what's still open. The two most consequential fixes — the top-plate splice reinforcement and the center-stile ceiling brackets — are now folded into the cut list and fastening decisions above.

Resolved

  • Out-of-plane tipping resistance (was Critical): the ladder frame was tied to the building only at its two end wall-cleats, 104" apart, with zero connection from the center stile / center 104" span to the ceiling — a push anywhere near the middle had no real load path back to structure, and the KALLAX units are deliberately not fastened to the stiles so they can't help. Screwing the top plate more firmly to the stiles doesn't fix this: the top plate is two pieces butt-spliced at the center stile (the exact point with the longest, weakest load path), so it can't act as a single stiff beam distributing a center-span load out to the two end cleats — the splice itself is the weak link, not the stile joints. Fix (corrected): an earlier version of this fix specified brackets "at the top of the center stile" — wrong, because the center stile stops at the top plate (~59 3/16" high) exactly like the other two stiles, and the ceiling above it is 73 1/4" (the center stile's x-position is past the ceiling step, so it's in the low zone, not the 82 1/8" tall zone). There is nothing between the top plate and the ceiling there except the upper panel itself. The actual fix: a third vertical blocking run (same 3/4" x 1 1/2" spec as the panel's seam blocking) at the center stile's x-position, running top plate to ceiling (~14 1/16"), with the two steel angle brackets at its top, not the stile's. Force path: a push on the divider center transfers through the KALLAX carcass (unfastened, so it only transmits contact force, not stiffness) into the top plate (one continuous member across all 3 stile joints) into this new blocking run into the ceiling bracket into a joist or drywall anchor. See "Fastening decisions" and the cut list.
  • Top plate splice unreinforced (was Critical): the splice lands on only 3/8" of stile edge grain on each side with nothing else at the most-loaded joint in the frame. Fix: a 3/4" plywood scab (~16" long) glued and screwed across the top of the splice. See the cut list.
  • Upper panel has no intermediate vertical support (was Important): a ~102"-wide sheet held only at its top and bottom edges (four cleat runs, no verticals between them) will bow over that span. Fix: vertical blocking (3/4" x 1 1/2") added behind each of the 2 seams, top plate to ceiling cleat — turns one ~102"-wide unsupported bay into three bays of roughly 27"/38"/38", each backed by the seam's own cap strip on the room-facing side. See geometry.js's UPPER_PANEL_SEAM_1/2 and elevation.js's drawUpperPanel().
  • Upper panel seams crossed the ceiling step (was Important): seams previously placed at flat 48"/96" marks crossed the ceiling step at ~28 3/4" from the opening's left edge, making one panel piece an L-shaped cutout with an unspecified sheet layout. Fix: seams moved to land exactly at the step and at the low zone's mid-span, so all three panel pieces (~27 1/4", ~37 21/32", ~37 21/32") are plain rectangles. This also gives the vertical blocking above a sensible place to land. See the updated cut list.
  • prompt.md vs. geometry.js disagreement (was Critical): prompt.md was the original handoff brief, written before field measurements — it now disagrees with the measured numbers in geometry.js on nearly every dimension (opening width, KALLAX sizes, stile length, upper panel material). Fix: prompt.md now carries an explicit stale-data warning at the top; geometry.js and this page are the only sources to cut from. See CLAUDE.md.
  • Stale hardcoded dimension labels in the elevation drawings (Minor): four height labels in elevation.js (base/carcass/ledge/upper-panel heights) were typed from the old prompt.md numbers instead of computed from the live constants like every other label on the page, so the bedroom/office elevations displayed measurements that quietly contradicted the cut list. Fix: all four now use inchLabel() against the same constants the cut list uses. Same fix applied to a stale divider-depth figure in framing.html's frame note.

Still open

  • Frame-to-opening fit (re-derived Sept 2026 — the old "1/8" of slack" crisis was an error): earlier versions of this page compared the frame's 104 1/16" width against the 104 3/16" floor reading and concluded there was 1/8" of total slack. That was wrong twice over: this opening has no casing, and the floor reading is taken between the baseboards, below the bottom plate, where it never constrains the frame. The real comparison is against bare wall-to-wall:
    opening, wall to wall 105 1/4"
    frame, stile to stile 104 1/16"
    slack 1 3/16" total = 19/32" per side
    That is workable but tight, and it is fully consumed by the side gap the baseboard requires — the frame + two gaps comes to exactly 105 1/4". Three things still to confirm before cutting:
    1. Baseboard projection, measured directly (currently derived — see "Measurement notes"). If it exceeds ~19/32", the frame does not clear it as drawn.
    2. Wall-to-wall at two more heights — just above the baseboard (~8–10" up) and near the ceiling. Same measurement already taken at KALLAX height, repeated for plumb. Use the narrowest reading as OPEN_W.
    3. Whether the 4x4 has bowed. Its width is measured at its widest point today (57 13/16" at the bottom vs 57 7/8" at the top); loaded particleboard can move further.
    If any of those eats the margin, the cheapest fix is to stop sizing the frame as a rigid sum of KALLAX widths: narrow the frame by 1/2"–5/8" and let the KALLAX units float in slightly oversized bays. They are infill and are deliberately not fastened to the stiles, so a small gap at each end is invisible once the backers and battens go on — and it converts the accumulated tolerance problem into a non-issue.
  • Assembly sequence isn't written down: the implied order (bottom plate → stiles → KALLAX infill → top plate → panel → backers → baseboard) has a real gap between "stiles standing" and "top plate on," where three unbraced plywood fins have nothing holding them plumb. In practice: set and shim the bottom plate, install both wall cleats, stand the end stiles and screw them to the cleats (this alone stabilizes them), then slide in the 4x3, stand the center stile against it, slide in the 4x4 — the two KALLAX boxes brace the center stile temporarily during assembly even though they're not fastened to it long-term — then add the top plate (splice scab included), then the ceiling brackets and upper panel. Worth writing into its own numbered section here once confirmed against the actual joist/stud measurements.
  • Fastener schedule is incomplete: "Fastening decisions" above now covers the frame joints, wall cleats, ceiling brackets, and panel blocking, but backer-to-KALLAX-carcass attachment, baseboard-to-plate attachment, and batten/cap-strip fastening (brads, presumably) are still unspecified.
  • Track light detail's feasibility math is undone: the plan places the upper panel's edge (locally, in depth) just past the junction box on the bedroom side — but this only works if the box actually projects far enough past the divider's bedroom face (more than half the panel's centered depth, ~7 3/4") to clear it. That arithmetic depends on the still-unmeasured box projection, and the box sits right at the ceiling step, where the panel's height profile is also changing — the interaction between the two hasn't been worked out. Treat the field measurement in "Open items" as blocking, not just a nice-to-have, and redo the depth check once it's in hand. If it doesn't clear, the previously-discarded cutout/curb/trim option is the fallback.
  • Joist direction and stud locations: both still unknown, and both are load-bearing facts now — joist direction/location determines where the center-stile ceiling brackets can actually land, and stud locations determine whether the wall cleats land where the design assumes.

Measurement notes

Field measurements (KALLAX units already in place against the walls) turned up a few small differences from the original nominal numbers. None of them threaten the fit — the assembly still clears the opening with real margin — but they're worth knowing about:

  • Both KALLAX units measure slightly larger than the IKEA spec sheet: 4x3 is 44" wide (spec: 43 7/8"), 4x4 is up to 57 13/16" wide at the bottom of the carcass (spec: 57 7/8"), and both are 15 7/16" deep (spec: 15 3/8") and up to 57 11/16" tall (spec: 57 5/8"). The cut list and every drawing on this site use these measured numbers.
  • There is no casing on this opening. The side walls are bare drywall corners at standing height; the only trim is the baseboard at the floor (confirmed visually — see photos/baseboard.jpg — and by direct inspection of both sides). Earlier versions of this page carried a "casing ~3/4" proud each side" assumption inherited from prompt.md, and treated the floor-level baseboard-to-baseboard reading as an "inside casing" constraint the frame had to fit inside. Both were wrong. See "Structural review findings".
  • Wall-to-wall at KALLAX height is 105 1/4" (corrected Sept 2026 — an earlier 106 1/8" reading was superseded). Once the casing assumption is dropped, the two field readings are consistent and their difference is simply the baseboard:
    wall to wall (bare drywall, at KALLAX top) = 105 1/4"
    baseboard face to baseboard face (at floor) = 104 3/16"
    difference ÷ 2 = ~17/32" — the baseboard's projection
    That derived ~17/32" is close to the 5/8" baseboard originally assumed in prompt.md, which is a good sign. The former "unexplained ~2" discrepancy" was an artifact of the superseded reading plus the phantom casing, and needs no explanation.
  • The frame's real width constraint is the bare wall-to-wall 105 1/4", not the floor reading — which is taken below the bottom plate and never constrains the frame. Against 105 1/4" the 104 1/16" frame has 1 3/16" of total slack, or 19/32" per side. Workable, but not generous: it pins the side gap almost exactly, leaving little freedom to choose it.
  • Open — measure directly before cutting cleats: the baseboard's projection is currently derived by subtracting two readings taken at different heights, which compounds both their errors into the single number the side gap depends on. Hook a tape on the baseboard face and measure back to the wall above it. If it comes in thicker than ~19/32", the frame does not clear it as drawn — see "Frame-to-opening fit" under "Structural review findings".
  • Ceiling step measured at 28 3/4" from the external wall (design used 28 1/2"); tall-side ceiling height 82 1/8" (design: 82"); low-side ceiling height 73 1/4"–73 1/2" across a few points (design: 73") — this site uses the low end (73 1/4") as the worst case.
  • Floor is pretty level across the run; the 4x4 wobbles slightly — check and shim when setting the bottom plate.
  • Still open / not yet measured: wall stud-to-drywall-face thickness (needed to know how far the assembly projects into each room), stud locations on both walls, and the track light junction box's projection past the wall plane.
